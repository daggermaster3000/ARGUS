# Counting AF488⁺ cells in squares: protocol

You will see 144 squares of tissue, each 120 × 120 µm, cut from slide-scanner images of assembloids. Count the
AF488-labelled cells in each square by placing **one dot per cell**. Both annotators count the same squares, each on
their own, so that we can measure how well two people agree and compare the automated count with yours.

**Do this alone.** Do not discuss squares or criteria with the other annotator until both of you have finished. The
squares are coded and shuffled: you are not told the genotype, experiment, section or what the automated method
found, and you should not try to find out.

## What counts as an AF488⁺ cell

Dot a structure when **all** of these hold:

1. **It is a cell body (soma):** a compact, roughly round or oval bright structure, about 5–15 µm across (the scale
   bar in the page is 10 µm). A labelled cell may have processes; dot its body once, not its processes.
2. **It is clearly brighter than the tissue around it** in the green (AF488) image. Use the brightness slider if you
   need to, but judge against the local background, not against the brightest cell in the square.
3. **It has a nucleus** in the blue (AF405) image when you switch it on (key **2**). A bright green blob with no
   nucleus under or beside it is usually a process, a fragment or debris: do not dot it.

**Do not dot:** processes, fibres or thin lines on their own; small dots or puncta (much smaller than a nucleus);
diffuse green haze; bright tissue edges or folds; anything outside the tissue.

**Touching or overlapping cells:** dot each cell body you can tell apart (one dot per nucleus with green around it).

**Faint cells:** dot a cell when you are reasonably sure it is a labelled cell body. If you are unsure, leave it out.
Try to apply the same rule in every square.

**Reference cells:** beside each square, a column shows the brightest labelled cells of the same section (same staining,
scan and display, and the same channel and brightness settings as the square). They show what a clearly positive cell
looks like in that section; they are not part of the square and are not counted. A cell does not need to be as bright as
the references to be dotted: compare its shape and its contrast with the tissue around it.

## The counting frame

Each square is shown with a 20 µm margin of context around it. Only the **inner frame** (the white rectangle) is
counted:

- dot a cell whose body is **mostly (more than half) inside the frame**;
- do not dot cells mostly outside it, even if they are visible in the margin.

## Using the page

Open `annotate.html` in Chrome, Edge or Firefox (from this folder) and enter your name. Then for each square:

| Action | How |
|---|---|
| place a dot | left click |
| remove a dot | right click on it |
| AF488 only / AF488 + nuclei / nuclei only | keys **1** / **2** / **3** |
| brightness, contrast | sliders above the image |
| square finished (also when it has no cells) | **Done** (or key **D**) |
| next / previous square | → / ← |

Your work is saved in the browser as you go. **At the end, and once or twice on the way, press "Export CSV"** and send
the file. If you change computer, "Import CSV" restores your work.

Expect about one minute per square, 2–3 hours in all. Take breaks; do not rush the last squares.
